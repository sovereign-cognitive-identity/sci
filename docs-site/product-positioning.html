Why Sci

The Unowned Market Nobody Else Protects

Reversible anonymization for LLMs is a gap the industry left open. We're filling it.

The Core Insight

Every PII tool on the market does the same thing: strip your data, send clean data to the LLM, get a response, and hope it still makes sense. When Presidio redacts "Alice" to "[PERSON]" and sends it to Claude, Claude can't say "Alice should do X" in the response—because it never learned who Alice was.

Reversible round-trip anonymization is different: anonymize outbound, have the LLM reason over the anonymous data, then restore the original entities back into the response. The model sees enough structure to think clearly. Your identity stays off the wire. The answer stays legible.

This capability is essentially unowned.

Why Memory Alone Won't Work for Sci

The agent memory market is crowded, VC-funded, and converging on a commodity feature set: conversation history, retrieval augmentation, fine-tuning. Competing there head-on as "another memory layer" is a losing fight for a small, open-source project.

But memory built on reversible anonymization as the foundation? That's defensible. That's new.

Why Self-Sovereign Identity Isn't Ready

Solid, DIDs, and decentralized identity have the right principles. They also have zero LLM provider integration, minimal mainstream adoption, and no obvious path to production for the next 18 months. Watch the space. Don't bet the product on it yet.

The Tailwinds Are Real

  • EU AI Act enforcement — regulators will force LLM providers to tighten data handling. Sci-first workflows suddenly look like compliance insurance.
  • GDPR pressure on LLM providers — the lawsuits are coming. Companies that can prove "the model never saw PII" have an asymmetric defense.
  • Local-first movement — Obsidian, Logseq, Replicache, and others are proving that users will adopt software that keeps their data off the cloud.
  • On-device inference — Apple Intelligence, Ollama, and quantized models shrink the surface area for data exposure. Sci fits the era.
