## Who needs a commercial license?

You need a commercial license if:

- You want to offer Sci (or a product built on Sci) as a **managed service to others** without open-sourcing your service stack
- You want to **embed Sci in a proprietary product** and distribute it without AGPL obligations
- You are an **enterprise** whose legal team will not accept AGPL terms

You do **not** need a commercial license if:

- You self-host Sci for your own personal or organizational use
- You run Sci as the managed tier operated by Sci Identity, Inc. (that's us — we operate under a commercial license)
- You build on Sci and open-source your modifications under AGPL-3.0
- You are an individual developer evaluating or contributing to the project

## What AGPL-3.0 requires of service operators

If you run Sci as a network service (e.g., a hosted managed memory service), AGPL-3.0 requires that you make the complete corresponding source code of your modified version available to users of that service.

This means: if you fork Sci and host it for others, you must open-source your fork under AGPL-3.0. This is the core protection for the Sci project — it prevents the codebase from being taken and commercialized without contributing back.

## FAQ

**Can I use Sci at my company internally without a commercial license?**

Yes, if you are self-hosting for internal use and not providing it as a service to external users, AGPL-3.0 permits this without a commercial license. Internal tooling is not "conveying" the software to the public.

**Can I contribute to Sci under AGPL and use my contributions in a commercial product?**

Contributions to the Sci project are made under AGPL-3.0. To use your own contributions in a commercial product without AGPL obligations, you would need a commercial license that covers your use case.

**Is this OSI-approved open source?**

Yes. AGPL-3.0 is an OSI-certified open source license. Sci is open source. The commercial license is an additional offering for use cases that require AGPL exemption.
